Research across community and genomic scales.

My PhD develops a connected framework for revealing hidden forest arthropod biodiversity: improving molecular detection, linking communities to forest structure, and moving to population and landscape genomics in a non-model soil arthropod.

PhD · ConFoBi B4

Hidden forest biodiversity

University of Freiburg · Southern Black Forest

My dissertation connects four manuscript chapters across ecological and genomic scales.

Chapter I

eDNA metabarcoding in tree-related microhabitats

Developed and evaluated an eDNA metabarcoding approach for arthropod communities associated with tree-related microhabitats, including how sampling substrate and marker choice affect recovery of cryptic biodiversity.

Chapter II

Forest structure as a filter of arthropod diversity

Linked molecularly detected arthropod communities with deadwood, tree-related microhabitats, canopy and broader forest structural conditions across 135 sampling points.

Chapter III

PoLoCo and pooled low-coverage genomics

Built a reproducible workflow connecting draft genome assembly and validation with Pool-seq mapping, SNP processing and allele-frequency analysis for small non-model invertebrates.

Chapter IV

Connectivity and genotype–environment associations

Used pooled genomic data from Entomobrya nivalis to investigate population structure, connectivity and genomic associations with vegetation, forest structure, management and soil conditions.
